What it would do

Every deadline, per vehicle and per driver

Registration, insurance, state inspections, and service due dates for each truck or van — plus driver licence and medical-card expiry dates where your drivers need them. You enter the dates (or import your spreadsheet); nothing has to be figured out from a rulebook.

Warnings at 30, 14, and 7 days — and when it’s overdue

Push and email warnings before a date arrives, a weekly digest of what’s coming up, and an overdue flag that stays until the item is marked done. A deadline tool that misses once is worthless, so the alert history is visible to you — you can see what was sent, and when.

A document vault for each vehicle

Registration, insurance cards, and inspection paperwork filed against the vehicle they belong to, so the document is in the same place as the deadline it renews.

Set up from the spreadsheet you already have

Import your existing list instead of re-typing it. And if you ever leave, the whole thing exports back out as a spreadsheet — your records stay yours.

What this is not

  • Not a fleet platform — no GPS, no telematics, no driver tracking, no cameras. Full suites already do that job; this only does the paperwork and the dates.
  • Not maintenance software — no work orders, parts, or fuel tracking. Service due dates are in; the shop-management system is not.
  • Not a compliance guarantee — it is a reminder tool. Your deadlines stay your responsibility; this just makes them much harder to lose.
  • No contract, no lock-in — the plan is month-to-month with one-tap cancellation, the opposite of the multi-year contracts small shops complain about in this category.

An honest note on spreadsheets

If you run a handful of trucks and your spreadsheet genuinely works, keep it — this would be overkill. The shops this is aimed at are the ones where the list has outgrown one person’s memory: dates staggered across the year, more than one person driving, and at least one deadline that has already slipped. If that’s not you, the waitlist isn’t either — and that’s useful for us to learn too.
